Companion reference

What operational problem would this technology solve for this application? “None” is a valid reason not to adopt it.

Key terms

Operational ownership
Who maintains, monitors, protects, and restores the service.
Recovery requirements
How quickly service must return and how much data loss is tolerable.
Total cost
Service charges plus engineering effort, support, risk, and maintenance.
Reconsideration trigger
An observed requirement or constraint that would justify changing the arrangement.

Starting points, not prescriptions

  • Static portfolio: static hosting and appropriate delivery; no inherent production container requirement.
  • Small WordPress/Drupal site: suitable shared or managed CMS hosting, with updates and tested backups.
  • Frontend plus API: evaluate PaaS, managed containers, serverless, or a managed VM against workload needs.
  • Growing SaaS: measure bottlenecks, improve releases and recovery, scale justified components.
  • Large distributed application: orchestration may help if workload coordination and team capacity justify it.
  • Significant uptime/compliance requirements: assess controls and recovery evidence with relevant specialists, regardless of traffic.

A short decision record

  1. What must the application do?
  2. What downtime and data loss are acceptable?
  3. Who owns operations and what can they sustain?
  4. Which option meets the requirements with manageable cost?
  5. What tradeoff are we accepting?
  6. What evidence would make us reconsider?

Compose, containers, IaC, managed hosting, and Kubernetes can each be adopted or declined independently. Learning Kubernetes does not create a requirement to operate it.
