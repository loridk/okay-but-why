## Governance

JULES: Security governance defines how an organization directs and oversees security.

PARISA: Policies.

Roles.

Accountability.

Risk appetite.

Standards.

JULES: Exactly.

PARISA: Who decides what acceptable risk means.

JULES: Right.


## Policy, Standard, Procedure, Guideline

PARISA: Security+ vocabulary.

JULES: **Policy** states management intent and requirements.

PARISA: “Company data must be protected.”

JULES: **Standard** makes requirements specific.

PARISA: “MFA required for privileged access.”

JULES: **Procedure** tells people how to perform a task.

PARISA: Steps for onboarding admin account.

JULES: **Guideline** recommends good practice but may be flexible.

PARISA: Companion notes, naturally.


## Risk Assessment

JULES: Identify assets, threats, vulnerabilities, likelihood, impact, controls.

PARISA: Quantitative if useful.

Qualitative if that’s more realistic.

JULES: Exactly.

PARISA: Annual loss expectancy and all the math?

JULES: Security+ may ask.

PARISA: Companion.


## Risk Responses

JULES: Avoid.

Transfer.

Mitigate.

Accept.

PARISA: Avoid: stop doing risky activity.

Transfer: insurance or contract shifts financial consequences.

Mitigate: controls reduce likelihood or impact.

Accept: knowingly live with it.

JULES: Exactly.

PARISA: Not “ignore.”

JULES: Acceptance should be informed and authorized.


## Risk Appetite and Tolerance

PARISA: Appetite: amount/type of risk organization is willing to pursue or retain.

JULES: Tolerance: acceptable variation around objectives.

PARISA: Business words security inherited.

JULES: Yep.


## Privacy

PARISA: Security and privacy overlap but are not identical.

JULES: Security asks whether data is protected from unauthorized harm.

Privacy asks broader questions about collection, use, consent, retention, sharing, rights.

PARISA: A perfectly encrypted database can still be a privacy nightmare if we never should have collected the data.

JULES: Exactly.


## Data Minimization

PARISA: Best secret is data you never collected.

JULES: Strong privacy and security principle.

PARISA: Less data means less breach impact.

JULES: Usually, yes.


## Classification

PARISA: Public.

Internal.

Confidential.

Restricted.

JULES: Organizations use classifications to drive handling requirements.

PARISA: Encryption, retention, access, sharing.

JULES: Exactly.


## Retention

PARISA: Keeping data forever feels easy.

JULES: Until breach, litigation, regulation, storage cost, privacy request.

PARISA: Retention schedules reduce unnecessary exposure.

JULES: Yes.


## Compliance

PARISA: PCI DSS.

HIPAA.

GDPR.

SOC 2.

JULES: Different things with different scopes and legal status.

PARISA: Don’t call all of them “certifications.”

JULES: Thank you.

PARISA: Compliance means meeting applicable requirements.

JULES: It does not guarantee every relevant security risk is handled.


## Third-Party Risk

PARISA: Vendor has our data.

JULES: Their security becomes part of our risk.

PARISA: Contracts.

Assessments.

Security requirements.

Breach notification.

Data-processing terms.

JULES: Exactly.


## Exceptions

PARISA: Policy says MFA everywhere.

Legacy system can’t support it.

JULES: Document exception, assess risk, implement compensating controls, assign expiration/review.

PARISA: Not “Steve said it’s fine.”

JULES: Preferably not.
